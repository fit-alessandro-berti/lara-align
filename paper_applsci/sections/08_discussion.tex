\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results are most useful when they guide a concrete choice about when to generate a candidate and what to infer from it. This section connects the three research questions to that choice, distinguishes the semantic contract from empirical performance, and identifies the changes needed for stronger claims. It also records limits of the evidence so that the observed transfer is not mistaken for general purpose alignment competence.

\subsection{Interpreting the Three Research Questions}

For RQ1, the experiments show that the decoder and replay pipeline produced legal candidates on all evaluated inputs, with 91.0\% optimality on the controlled test split. \begin{revised}Optional single-log repair raises the reference checkpoint's rate to 97.3\%, while leaving 14 suboptimal candidates.\end{revised} The formal guarantee is conditional: when replay accepts, the candidate is a complete executable explanation and its cost is an upper bound. The observed 100\% acceptance rate does not prove that the bounded decoder always reaches the final marking. Larger gaps on stress inputs and the worked failure show that a reliable legality check can coexist with a weak candidate policy.

For RQ2, machine learning has a measurable but concentrated effect. The duplicate prefix improvement demonstrates that global trace context can help decide among equal labels before the decoder reaches their distinguishing continuation. The identifier renaming experiment shows more stable candidate costs than the name ordered baseline. The remaining motifs show no cost improvement. The evidence therefore supports using learned rankings for selected ambiguities, rather than attributing the overall success rate to the neural network.

\begin{revised}For RQ3, fixed checkpoints accept new Petri net sizes and label vocabularies without fine tuning. The structural transfer experiment excludes training-isomorphic graphs and tests a cyclic class absent from training under two compilers. It establishes measured behavior on those structural shifts, but the inconsistent guidance gains do not support broad learned superiority. Candidate quality degrades on larger stress inputs, and real log results do not show an advantage from learned rankings. Table~\ref{tab:revision-timing} supplies comparable per-trace timings on the controlled test set; it does not convert a feasible candidate into an exact result or demonstrate faster certification. Reuse of parameters, successful replay, and improved learned decision quality remain separate empirical claims.\end{revised}

\subsection{What a Verified Candidate Can and Cannot Tell an Analyst}

A verified candidate is useful because it is an executable explanation of the observed case. It can support visualization, an initial inspection of deviations, and a bound on the best achievable cost. Its deviation locations should nevertheless be read in the light of its optimization status. The fitting receipt model makes this concrete: a positive candidate cost does not prove that the trace deviates from the model, because a zero cost alignment may exist. If the goal is to assert the minimum deviation count, exact evidence remains necessary.

Likewise, equal optimal costs do not identify a unique diagnostic story. Different optimal alignments may allocate deviations or synchronize independent activities differently. Certification establishes minimal cost under the chosen policy, not the uniquely correct account of what happened in the organization. Business interpretation still depends on the model, event data quality, and the suitability of the cost function.

Progressive presentation is a practical application of these distinctions. A workbench can show a candidate and its replay status immediately, then add exact comparison when available. The implementation supports that sequence. A deployment policy that starts exact search first and invokes candidate generation only for slow cases could reduce unnecessary inference, but this policy has not been evaluated. The threshold would depend on model structure, hardware, expected quality, and the analyst's need for a certificate.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:validity}

\begin{revised}\textbf{Training distribution.} The corpus is small and intentionally structured. It contains four motif types, three of which use fixed controlled constructions. Family level splitting prevents separation of a generated family's views, but it does not exclude repeated visible languages or motifs across splits. The structural test explicitly excludes graphs isomorphic to training inputs and holds out a cyclic class, with a second compiler. Both compilers receive generated process trees, and the test spans only 128 trees. It is not a sample of unrestricted industrial Petri nets. Training on abstract labels also leaves business semantics, resources, and time outside the study.\end{revised}

\begin{revised}\textbf{Teacher and objective.} Exact targets are verified for the supplied cost model, but only one optimal witness is retained. Sequence agreement therefore mixes candidate quality with witness choice. The masking rule for duplicate prefix targets is motif specific. The matched loss ablation measures the effect of supervising the log head on greedy decoding for two seeds; optional repair uses that head to order alternatives. Neither experiment establishes an objective aligned with minimum decoded cost, and the auxiliary region losses remain unablated. Model scores are global transition preferences rather than marking- and position-specific action values.\end{revised}

\begin{revised}\textbf{Statistical coverage.} The full objective is evaluated with three training seeds; the loss ablation has two matched pairs. Reporting all selected checkpoints and their sample standard deviation reveals variation in these runs, but three seeds cannot tightly characterize arbitrary retraining. The seed-13 run uses a different execution environment from the runs for seeds 17 and 29. Family-bootstrap intervals condition on each fitted checkpoint and generator; they are not intervals over training seeds or deployment domains. The stress cells contain only ten inputs. The held-out real log study covers three logs, including one restricted road traffic sample, with a single case split per log. Perfect replay coverage in these collections should not be generalized to arbitrary systems.\end{revised}

\begin{revised}\textbf{Comparison protocol.} The evaluation uses one exact backend and concrete approximation configurations. Other exact implementations, window sizes, or representative subsets may change the results. The timers in the reference experiments cover different operations and provide descriptive measurements. The common timing harness includes method-specific preparation, conversion, and replay, with warmup, repeated calls, and full coverage accounting. Its comparison is limited to per-trace implementations on the controlled test set; the batch subset method and real-log evaluations are outside that protocol. Internal checks remain part of each implementation's measured cost. Different hardware, software, concurrency, or target processes may change the runtime ordering.\end{revised}

\begin{revised}\textbf{Real log interpretation.} The models in the reference real log experiment use the same cases for discovery and alignment. The held-out study discovers models from separate cases and evaluates all held-out variants, but uses the same source log and may share variants across the case split. This tests transfer to unseen cases, not compliance with an independent organizational specification. Variant and case weighting answer different questions. Both are reported, and the limited road traffic sample and absence of duplicate visible labels remain explicit.\end{revised}

\textbf{Semantic and computational scope.} The evaluated setting uses ordinary control flow Petri nets and fixed unit visible deviation costs. Neural features omit explicit arc weights, current decoder markings, and data guards. The scorer uses dense attention, and the decoder's depth bounds do not bound the width of marking exploration. Neither component has a demonstrated constant latency guarantee. The guarantee that survives these implementation limits is the conditional replay/certification contract.

\subsection{Methodological Extensions Suggested by the Evidence}

\begin{revised}The optional repair procedure addresses the failure in Figure~\ref{fig:failure} by explicitly testing log alternatives, and its measured gains justify further investigation of candidate exploration. Its trials each force only one log position; the remaining continuation is greedy. A decoder could instead rank synchronous, log, and model alternatives jointly and retain several partial continuations. Recomputing scores from the current marking or predicting model preferences per event position could help it respond to earlier choices. These extensions would need separate evaluation against unguided search and strong classical approximations, including their extra computation.\end{revised}

A separate extension concerns certification. A candidate verified by replay supplies a feasible incumbent cost. An exact search algorithm can potentially use that cost for pruning while relying on admissible lower bounds for correctness. Such an integration would need to expose the relevant solver interface, preserve exactness, and demonstrate reduced total work. Merely comparing a candidate with an independently computed optimum, as done here, cannot support a certification speedup claim.

Training could also focus on the decisions that the active decoder actually makes, collect multiple optimal witnesses, or penalize downstream candidate gaps more directly. Additional structural diversity and deliberate tests of hash relabeling would strengthen evidence for reuse. The auxiliary regions would require independent validation before being treated as meaningful process decompositions. These are research directions motivated by observed limitations, not capabilities of the evaluated checkpoint.
