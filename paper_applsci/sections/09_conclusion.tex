\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The aim of this study is to determine a useful role for reusable machine learning in alignment based conformance checking while keeping the meaning of an explanation explicit. LARA combines a pretrained scorer for graphs and traces with a decoder that tracks the marking, independent replay, and optional exact comparison. The scorer proposes move preferences; replay establishes executability and an upper bound; successful exact computation establishes the optimum or supplies a replacement.

\begin{revised}Across three training seeds, guided greedy decoding attains mean optimality of 90.76\% with sample standard deviation .30 percentage points on the controlled synthetic test set, compared with 86.91\% without guidance. The reference checkpoint gives 466 rather than 445 optima, and optional single-log repair raises this to 498 of 512. The matched loss ablation provides no evidence that supervising the unused log head helps greedy decoding. Tests excluding training-isomorphic graphs and including cyclic models under two compilers reveal inconsistent learned gains. Held-out cases from three real logs show no quality advantage from guidance. All evaluated candidates pass replay, but these coverage observations do not prove completeness of bounded decoding. The reference greedy policy ranks fifth among six methods in Table~\ref{tab:approxsmall}; common-boundary timings also show it slower than exact search on the small test set. The current certified mode still runs exact search for every trace.\end{revised}

The resulting contribution is a transparent, reproducible study of learned candidate scoring within an alignment pipeline whose guarantees come from process semantics. The most promising next steps are stronger decisions among competing move types, broader tests of structural transfer, and using verified incumbents to reduce exact search work. Their value must be demonstrated in candidate quality and total computation, with the same explicit separation between a plausible prediction and a proven alignment property.
